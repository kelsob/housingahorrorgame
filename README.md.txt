README – Working Title: LifePod
High Concept

LifePod is a short dystopian survival game set in a near-future megacorp hellscape where rent, employment, and citizenship are indistinguishable.

The player has been “promoted” into the role of LifePod Synergy Manager, meaning they are responsible for managing roommates in their assigned corporate housing unit. This promotion is framed as an opportunity, but in reality it is an unpaid burden designed to increase “tenant synergy” by a marginal corporate-validated percentage.

The goal is simple: survive 5 days without being financially, physically, or corporately erased.

The apartment is not a home. It is an extension of the workplace.

Core Gameplay Loop

Each day consists of:

Morning

Manage apartment (thermostat, food, furniture, roommates)

Perform survival actions (sell plasma, sell items, gamble)

Spend energy

Day

Go to work (costs energy)

Earn money

Missing work escalates penalties

Night

Roommate events trigger

Sleep (partial energy restore, never full)

Systems apply consequences

Core Systems
Energy

Max 10

Never fully restored

Sleep restores partial energy

Food restores small energy

Certain roommates reduce sleep quality

Money

Earned from work

Spent on rent, food, thermostat

Can be gained via plasma selling or gambling

Rent

Paid daily

Failure to pay triggers eviction escalation

Two missed payments = failure state

Thermostat

Adjustable AC level

Higher AC = better sleep, higher cost

Lower AC = cheaper, worse sleep, possible food spoilage, roommate dissatisfaction

Furniture

Sellable items: Bed, Couch, TV, Fridge

Each affects roommates differently

Selling items can trigger roommate departure or conflict

Plasma Selling

Costs energy

Provides money

Overuse triggers medical failure state

Gambling

Simple 50/50 system

Double or lose money

Roommates

Roommates are modular pressure systems.

Each roommate:

Has 1 core demand

Has 1 passive effect

Can trigger 1 unique failure or event path

Examples:

Loud Night Disturber (reduces sleep)

Thief (steals money)

TV Addict (leaves if TV sold)

Seductive Manipulator (offers risky advantage)

Suspicious Food Vendor (unlocks cheaper food with hidden downside)

Roommates exist to:

Increase tension

Force tradeoffs

Turn the apartment into a management problem

Win / Lose Conditions
Win

Survive 5 days

Maintain employment

Avoid catastrophic system failure

Fail States

Miss 3 days of work

Energy reaches 0

Rent unpaid twice

Plasma overuse

Roommate-triggered death event

Tone

Corporate propaganda language

Cold optimism masking systemic cruelty

Satirical but oppressive

No moral redemption arc

The “promotion” is manipulation

The central theme:
Control is an illusion. Responsibility is outsourced downward.

Scope Constraints

Short experience (30–60 minutes)

Limited number of roommates (3–5 minimum viable)

Minimal dialogue

Systems-driven tension

Finite 5-day structure
